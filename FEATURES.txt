STELLENBOSCH WARD MAP
An interactive ward atlas for Stellenbosch Municipality

WHY THIS EXISTS
===============

Residents ask three questions constantly: Which ward am I in? Who is my
councillor and how do I reach them? Where do I vote? Today those answers
live in a PDF map, a contact list on another page, and the IEC's website.
Staff answer them by hand, over and over.

This map answers all three in one click, on a phone, without anyone
having to phone the switchboard. It already exists, it already works
against live official data, and it costs nothing to run.


WHAT IT DOES
============

One page, two views
-------------------
The all-wards overview shows every one of the 23 wards at once. Hover a
ward and it lifts out of the map with its number, councillor and
population; click it and you are in the detail view for that ward. Each
ward has its own shareable link (?ward=11), so a councillor can put a
direct link to their own ward on a poster, a WhatsApp group or a
newsletter, and it opens straight to the right place.

Ward detail
-----------
The single-ward view draws the ward boundary, every voting district
inside it, and every voting station as a clickable marker with its name,
district number, address and station type. Alongside it sits the ward's
demographic profile and its councillor's name and phone number.

Ward Insights: the map as an analysis tool
------------------------------------------
This is the part that goes beyond a wall map. Choose any of 15 metrics
and the whole municipality recolours as a live choropleth:

  Population & space   population density, total population, ward area,
                       working-age share, population diversity
  Economy              unemployment, lower-income households (<= R3 200)
  Education (20+)      no matric, matriculated, higher education
  Housing              informal dwellings, formal (brick/concrete)
  Population groups    Black African, Coloured, White

Three colour schemes, and a choice between continuous shading and
five-class quantile classification for metrics with a long tail. The
legend redraws itself to match. In seconds you can see where informal
housing concentrates, which wards carry the highest unemployment, or how
population density varies across the municipality: the kind of picture
that usually takes a GIS request and a two-week turnaround.

Ward demographics at a glance
-----------------------------
Every ward gets a panel of census charts: its share of the municipal
population, population group, employment, age structure, education for
adults 20+, monthly income, and dwelling type. Charts are drawn as clean
SVG, sized to fit, and every breakdown is completed to a full 100% so
nothing reads as a misleading part-total. Area and population density
are computed from the actual ward geometry.

Print-ready PDF export, one click
---------------------------------
Not a screenshot. The map composes a proper A4 landscape document:
municipal logo and header band, the ward number and councillor details,
the demographic charts down the left, and the map itself re-rendered to
fit the page exactly as framed on screen. Legend and data-source credits
are placed on the map. Ward councillors get a presentable handout for a
public meeting without a designer, and without a request to
Communications.

Map controls built for the job
------------------------------
Zoom, drag-a-box zoom-to-area, and recentre. A base-layer switch between
street map and satellite imagery, with boundary colours that
automatically recolour for contrast on each. Layer toggles for ward
boundaries, voting districts and voting stations. Labels place
themselves inside each polygon and step aside when they would collide,
so the map stays legible at every zoom level.


WHY IT CAN BE TRUSTED
=====================

Official data, fetched live
---------------------------
Ward boundaries come directly from the Municipal Demarcation Board's
official 2026 hosted Feature Service, queried at page load. When the MDB
republishes, the map follows. It is already serving the 2026
demarcation, including the wards that were materially redrawn.

Voting districts and stations come live from the Electoral Commission's
own GIS API. Nothing is a stale local copy that quietly rots.

Built to degrade, not to break
------------------------------
If the IEC API is slow or down, the ward map still renders and a plain
notice explains what is missing. Ward data loads even when the
reference data does not. A failure in a secondary source never takes the
page down: an important property for a public-facing service that has to
work during an election period.

Honest about its limits
-----------------------
A first-visit notice tells every user plainly that the site is
unofficial, that the IEC is still updating its voting district data, and
that anything official should be confirmed with the relevant authority.
No overclaiming.


WHAT IT COSTS TO RUN
====================

Nothing, effectively. It is a static site: HTML, one stylesheet, one
JavaScript file and a little JSON. No server, no database, no framework,
no build pipeline, no monthly platform fee, no dependency treadmill. It
will host on any existing municipal web space, or free on GitHub Pages.
There is no runtime for anyone to patch and nothing to be breached: the
site holds no user data, no logins, and no forms.

Embedding it is one link or one iframe. It works on a phone, and it
works on the ten-year-old desktop in a ward office.


WHAT THE MUNICIPALITY WOULD GET
===============================

- Fewer switchboard calls asking "which ward am I in" and "who is my
  councillor".
- A per-ward link every councillor can share directly with residents.
- A voting-station finder ready before the next local election.
- Ward-level demographic comparison for planning and reporting, without
  a GIS request queue.
- A printable ward one-pager on demand.
- All of it on official MDB and IEC data, updating itself.


WHAT I AM PROPOSING
===================

Adopt it. I built this as a resident, on public data, at no cost to the
Municipality, and I am offering it to Stellenbosch to use.

The work I would like to do with the Municipality's help:

1. Endorsement and a home. Let it live on a municipal URL so residents
   can trust what they are looking at.
2. Councillor data. A maintained contact list so the map is never out of
   date after a by-election.
3. Demographics on the 2026 boundaries. The census figures currently
   shown are aggregated to the previous ward footprints. With access to
   sub-place-level data these can be re-aggregated properly onto the new
   wards.
4. Whatever else is useful. Refuse collection days, ward committee
   meeting venues, project locations: the map is built to take more
   layers.

It works today. Open it and see.
